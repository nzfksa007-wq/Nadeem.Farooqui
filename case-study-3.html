Case Study 3: Working Capital Optimization & Dead Stock Recovery

Identifying and recovering capital locked in excess and obsolete spare parts inventory

← Back to Portfolio

1. Define: The Business Problem

Situation

A spare parts distributor had significant capital tied up in excess, slow moving and obsolete inventory with no clear recovery strategy or visibility into working capital impact.

Impact

  • AED 0.14M+ locked in dead stock with no movement
  • Lost opportunity cost on tied up capital
  • High carrying costs for slow moving inventory
  • Warehouse space consumed by non-productive inventory
  • No structured approach to recovery and disposal

Objective

Identify recovery opportunities and implement working capital optimization strategy to release cash tied up in non-productive inventory.

2. Measure: Current State Analysis

Baseline Metrics

Dead Stock Value
AED 0.14M
Total Inventory Value
AED 0.47M
Dead Stock %

Recovery Potential

Data Collection

  • Comprehensive inventory value analysis by SKU
  • Consumption pattern analysis (36 month history)
  • Identification of non moving and slow moving items
  • Age analysis of dead stock (years without movement)
  • Condition assessment for recovery viability

3. Analyze: Root Cause Identification

Root Causes Identified

  • Overstocking: Safety stock levels much higher than actual demand
  • Product Obsolescence: Technology/product changes made items irrelevant
  • Forecast Errors: Procurement based on inaccurate demand projections
  • No Usage Review: Items purchased but never consumed after initial order
  • Weak Inventory Controls: Lack of periodic dead stock identification

Data Analysis

Analysis showed 100+ SKUs with zero movement for 24+ months, with concentrated value in just 8-10 items. Estimated recovery potential of AED 42K (30% of dead stock value) through salvage, donation or disposal.

4. Improve: Solutions & Implementation

1. Dead Stock Identification & Assessment

  • Complete inventory audit with age and condition assessment
  • Classification into recovery, donation, disposal categories
  • Market value assessment for potential salvage

2. Recovery Strategy

  • Direct sales to secondary market suppliers
  • Donation opportunities (tax benefits)
  • Proper disposal and regulatory compliance
  • Write off procedures and accounting impact

3. Prevention of Future Dead Stock

  • Quarterly dead stock reviews and aging analysis
  • Slow moving inventory early warning system
  • Tighter demand forecasting and procurement controls
  • SAP reporting for automated dead stock identification

4. Working Capital Optimization

  • Safety stock right sizing by SKU criticality
  • Inventory carrying cost analysis and reduction
  • Cash flow impact monitoring and forecasting

5. Control: Sustaining Results

Control Measures

  • Monthly Dead Stock Monitoring: Aging analysis and movement tracking
  • Quarterly Dead Stock Reviews: Formal assessment and recovery planning
  • Procurement Controls: Demand validation before purchase orders
  • Inventory Carrying Cost Tracking: Impact of working capital optimization

Sustained Results

Recovered Capital
AED 42K

20%

<5%/year

Key Takeaways

  • AED 42K+ recovered through systematic dead stock identification and recovery
  • Working capital optimization improved cash flow and financial performance
  • Warehouse space freed up for productive inventory
  • Prevention mechanisms prevent future accumulation of dead stock
  • Quarterly reviews ensure sustained reduction of non productive inventory
