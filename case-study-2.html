Case Study 2: Stock Out Reduction & Demand Planning

Reducing critical stock outs through demand visibility, forecasting and safety stock optimization

← Back to Portfolio

1. Define: The Business Problem

Situation

A critical spare parts distributor was experiencing frequent stock outs affecting customer service levels. Emergency procurement costs were high, and demand forecasting was reactive rather than proactive.

Impact

  • Stock out rate of 2.0% affecting customer delivery commitments
  • High emergency procurement and expedited shipping costs
  • Poor demand visibility and reactive planning
  • Unpredictable safety stock levels causing overstock on some items
  • Customer dissatisfaction and lost business due to unavailability

Objective

Reduce stock out rate to below 1.5% through demand forecasting, safety stock optimization and inventory replenishment planning.

3. Analyze: Root Cause Identification

Root Causes Identified

  • Inadequate Safety Stock: Levels not aligned with demand variability and lead times
  • Poor Demand Visibility: Forecasts based on historical averages, not trends
  • Variable Lead Times: Supplier inconsistency not factored into replenishment planning
  • Reactive Ordering: Orders placed after stock outs occur, not proactively
  • No Demand Forecasting Model: Lack of systematic approach to predict future needs

Data Analysis

Analysis revealed that 70% of stock outs occurred in fast moving items (>100 units/month) with variable demand patterns. Lead time variability of +/- 30% was not being accounted for in safety stock calculations.

4. Improve: Solutions & Implementation

1. Demand Forecasting Model

  • 36 month consumption trend analysis
  • Seasonal pattern identification and adjustment
  • Forecast accuracy benchmarking and monitoring
  • Monthly forecast updates based on actual demand

2. Safety Stock Optimization

  • Safety stock calculated based on demand variability and lead time
  • Service level targets defined (95% vs 99% availability)
  • ABC SKU classification for differentiated service levels
  • Quarterly safety stock reviews and adjustments

3. Replenishment Planning

  • Reorder points calculated from forecast and safety stock
  • Proactive order placement before stock out risk
  • Lead time management and supplier coordination
  • Automated alerts for low stock conditions

4. Performance Monitoring

  • Real time stock availability tracking
  • Stock out incident analysis and root cause follow up
  • Forecast accuracy metrics and dashboard

5. Control: Sustaining Results

Control Measures

  • Daily Monitoring: Stock level alerts and replenishment order status
  • Weekly Reviews: Stock out incidents and root cause follow up
  • Monthly Forecasts: Updated demand forecasts based on actual consumption
  • Quarterly Safety Stock Review: Adjustments based on demand variability trends

Key Takeaways

  • Stock out rate reduced by 47% through demand forecasting and safety stock optimization
  • Service levels improved to 98.9% with consistent availability
  • Emergency procurement costs reduced significantly
  • Customer satisfaction improved with reliable stock availability
  • Demand planning moved from reactive to proactive methodology
